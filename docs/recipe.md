# Recipe: where River (online ML) and language models create value at the edge

A living document and one of the experiment's deliverables. Every line carries an **evidence tag**:
`dev` = seen on development seeds only; `locked` = confirmed on locked seeds 101-105 (none yet);
`untested`. Nothing here is a verdict until `locked`. Source of truth for numbers: `JOURNAL.md`, `reports/`.

## 1. Decide per stream whether forecasting is worth it
Measure skill against persistence first (it is free and often hard to beat). `dev`:
- Smooth, strongly structured streams (temperature): a simple online linear model beats persistence by 5 % at 10 s, 15 % at 1 min, 20 % at 10 min, 35-40 % at 1 h.
- Gusty or oscillating streams (wind, vibration RMS): 18-27 % at seconds to minutes.
- Random-walk-like streams with unpredictable regime changes (pressure with fronts): nothing beyond about a minute. Do not spend compute on it.
- Streams dominated by sudden cliffs (solar with cloud edges): every *learned* model lost to persistence (-10 to -24 %). A parameter-free geometric correction does not: scale the present value by the ratio of a clear-sky shape between now and the horizon (`clearsky_adapt`, noon taken from yesterday's curve), +1 to +3 % from 30 s to 5 min, `dev` seeds 1-3; a fixed noon loses 1 % after a seasonal phase shift. Small, but the only gain on this stream. A linear learner on the residual of that forecast still loses 7-25 %: do not train on cloud cliffs. A robust loss (median or Huber, target scaled) turns that into about +-2 % but not better than the parameter-free shape (`dev` seeds 1-3, 20x the cost): nothing learnable is left in the residual.

## 2. Pick the cheapest model that clears the bar
Standardise, clip features (spikes must not blow up a linear learner), predict **increments** (y[t+h] - y[t]) so an untrained model equals persistence, and use a stable learner (Bayesian ridge, `dev`). Per-step cost `dev`: persistence 1 us, linear 55-75 us, Hoeffding adaptive tree 110-520 us, SNARIMAX about 1 ms for +1 % at short horizons. Trees collapse on solar and pressure and crash on constant input. Complexity has not paid anywhere yet.

## 3. Treat adaptation as something to prove, not assume
`dev`: online beat frozen at long horizons on temperature (about +6 to +10 points of skill at 10 min to 1 h). Drift detectors watching the absolute error with reset or challenger replacement hurt badly at 1 min and above (error variance moves with the time of day; a fresh model cannot relearn a diurnal pattern in 10 minutes). Use forgetting or volatility-normalised signals instead: tried (`lin_rls`, `lin_rls_slow`, `lin_rls_vn`), see below.
**C4 / H5 verdict on the dev drift runs (seeds 1-2, `dev`): not supported.** No model passes the pre-registered rule (>= 15 % better in the hour after a regime change, within 3 % otherwise) on temperature, pressure, wind or vibration on either seed. Where online beats a model frozen after 2 days (temperature at 10 min to 1 h, a few percent on wind), it does so in stationary hours as much as in event windows, because the frozen model is just stale; in the hour after an event the online learners are mostly equal or worse. Against a reference retrained daily instead, there is still no event-specific gain (it is negative in 75-85 % of rows). Detector variants (`lin_lag_adwin`, `lin_lag_reset`) and the soft boost (`lin_rls_vn`) are the worst rows. Practical rule: use plain online linear learning (or forgetting) to keep a model from going stale, not to react to events; measure adaptation against a periodically retrained model, not a frozen one, or you will credit freshness to adaptation. Few events per stream (1-7) and two seeds: wide CIs; see `reports/dev-drift-c4.md`.

## 4. Outages: design the edge so it does not need the uplink
`dev`: the edge forecaster is unaffected by Skylink; the base side loses real-time forecasts during an outage (coverage 0-12 %) and 21-35 % of them in the 10 minutes after link-up. Feeding backfill in arrival order costs about nothing in error when history is addressed by absolute time (MAE within 3 % of the edge); waiting for a clean time-ordered replay costs coverage (65 % vs 79 %), not accuracy. Rule: key history by event time, tag replayed data, never let hindcasts count as real-time forecasts. Recovery after link-up (C3, `dev`, seeds 1-3): the three backfill strategies recover alike, because after link-up all three see the same live data. Of 19 stream-horizon rows, the base arm meets the C3 rule (median <= 15 min) in 4-6, the edge arm at the same instants (it never sees the outage) in 12-15; the metric has a noise floor (the edge "fails" too), but the outage clearly slows recovery and backfill handling does not help. If fast recovery after link-up matters, forecast at the edge.

## 5. Where language models fit (they are not forecasters)
- **Triage of alerts** (fault vs real event vs replay artefact). `dev`: zero-shot, no model beat a three-line rules baseline (best 0.60 vs 0.63 macro-F1; 1B models answer one class). With three worked examples per class in the prompt, gemma-4-e4b (4B) reached 0.76 (+0.13 over rules, still short of the preregistered bar). Decision models (Jev 0.8B) were fast (0.16 s), always valid, but not calibrated on numeric context. Reasoning models are too slow for alerts (qwen3:4b about 97 s per case). Five-model test (`reports/five-models-2026-10-20.md`, `dev`, 300 cases, one run): with three examples per class gpt-oss-20b reached 0.82 [0.78, 0.86] and Qwen3-14B 0.80 [0.75, 0.84], both above gemma-4-e4b; zero-shot only Qwen3-14B edged past the rules (0.68 [0.63, 0.73]); Ministral 3 14B and LFM2 24B-A2B stayed below the rules. Thinking on made Qwen3-14B no better (16/30 vs 21/30) at 108 s vs 2 s per case.
- **Model selection**: `untested` live; a scripted agent that always proposes the wrong model lost 11 % even behind guardrails. The agent must beat a "pick the best trailing MAE" rule, not nothing. Live, closed loop on all five streams (`dev` seeds 1-2, 3 days): qwen3:8b cut macro MAE by 1.6 and 3.9 % against the fixed model, the trailing-MAE rule by 5.0 and 4.2 %; both win on solar and lose on pressure (up to 17 % worse), so neither passes C5b. gemma3:4b never proposed a switch. The 8B model answers in about a minute (p95 2.5 min), so it is a slower copy of the rule, not better than it: use the rule.
- **Notes and explanations**: `untested`.
- **Escalation to a big brain** (C5e, `dev`, `reports/escalation-dev-2026-10-24.md`): Claude triaged 200 blind edge alerts at 0.935 two-class F1 against 0.71 for the 4B edge model and 0.75-0.76 for 14-20B local models; escalating every alert costs about 400 bytes a question and lifts the edge by +0.23, but the edge's disagreement triggers catch only a third of its errors, so cheap triggers buy +0.02 to +0.07. Claude's answers boiled down to five rules that score 0.912 on 3079 held-out alerts when run at the edge. Recipe: escalate to learn (rules and examples sent down), escalate the residue, never block on the answer. Design: `docs/escalation.md`.
- **Retrospective review of an hour** (job R, C5d, owner's idea 2026-10-24: models analyse results instead of selecting). `dev`, rules only so far: once the window is over, a handful of hindsight numbers made by a script (is the alert visible in true time order, how long did it last, did the value freeze, is a wind jump beyond what a gust can do) label the alerts at macro-F1 0.943 [0.90, 0.97] on 40 windows of seed 3 (0.925 on all 1158 alerts), against 0.68 for the triage rules that only see the first two minutes. Hindsight did the work, not a model. Model methods (per alert, whole window, rules plus model as writer) are `untested`; the bar for lift is now about 0.99, so the realistic role left is writing the uplink summary on top of the rules, checked in code.
Recipe so far: rules first for what rules can decide; a small model only for the residue; few-shot examples written from reviewed cases; every output validated in code; the model proposes, guardrails decide.

Model choice for these jobs (general comparison of fifteen local model runs on an Air and a Mini, one run each, `dev`): honesty ("I don't have that" instead of an invented number) separates models more than size does; qwen3:8b was the best all-rounder, gemma-4-12b the most careful, llama3.1:8b and phi4-mini the fastest but they invent; below about 3B nothing is reliable on grounded questions. Thinking models need a token budget well above 3000 or they return nothing. Add (`dev`, four more models, two repeats per prompt): Qwen3-14B MLX 4-bit and Ministral 3 14B pass the false-paper and no-data prompts but all four invent sister-vessel history when told the service is down; LFM2 24B-A2B invents on almost everything yet was the only one that turned the "find a non-existent hippo" question into an existence check. For triage with examples take Qwen3-14B (8 GB) or, if memory allows, gpt-oss-20b (12 GB, free memory fell to 16 %); never trust either for facts.

## 6. Guardrails that are cheap and worked in tests
Action space fixed in advance; one change per 30 min; reject proposals worse than the incumbent by 50 %; automatic rollback when the next 10 minutes are 20 % worse; log every call with input, output, latency, verdict. Count invalid outputs as failures.

## 7. Footprint to budget for `dev` (Air VM and Mini)
River service for five streams, ten models, base arms at 60x: roughly 0.3-0.45 of a core, about 130 MB. Local model servers dominate memory: a 4B model needs about 3-7 GB, Jev 0.8B about 5 GB resident (MLX), a 20B model 12 GB. On a 16 GB machine that already holds a 6 GB Docker VM, run one model at a time and never during family use.
